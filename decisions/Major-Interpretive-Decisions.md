# Major Interpretive Decisions

This file records only material interpretive decisions that are likely to be reopened in later translation or audit work. It is not a research notebook and does not replace the Glossary. Each entry names the confirmed text and line, the date of the decision when known, and a short reason. Where the original decision date is not securely recorded, the entry says **affirmed** with the date on which the settled decision was re-examined.

- **Creed Line 1 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `mhaymnīnan` is rendered **“we are faithful”**, not “we believe.” The project deliberately understands the faith-language here as lived faithfulness rather than reducing it to intellectual assent.
- **Creed Line 3 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `ʾīḥīḏāyā` is rendered **“the Only One.”** This preserves the Syriac expression itself rather than importing the conventional English “Only-Begotten.”
- **Creed Line 7 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `bar kyānā dʾaḇūhy` is rendered **“Son of the Nature of His Father.”** The translation preserves the Syriac conceptual wording rather than replacing it with Greek-derived technical equivalents such as “consubstantial.”
- **Creed Lines 5 and 11 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `ʾeṯīleḏ` is rendered **“begotten”** both of the Father and of Mariam the Virgin. The same Syriac lexeme is kept stable; distinct Syriac vocabulary for birth and nativity remains available for those concepts.
- **Our Father Line 12 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `lā taʿlan lnesyōnā` is rendered **“Do not enter us into testing.”** “Enter” is used causatively, as when someone is entered into an event, while “lead” is reserved for Syriac vocabulary more directly concerned with leading, walking, or guiding.
- **Ferial Slotha d'Sapra I, Line 6 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `panā` is rendered **“render.”** This preserves the return/give-back/grant breadth of the verb and avoids collapsing it into the project’s other vocabulary for “answer.”
- **Ferial Slotha d'Sapra II, Line 2 — decided 2026-08-24:** `s̈āḡdān` is rendered **“prostrate”** in **“They kneel and prostrate.”** The bodily force of `s-g-d` is made explicit here without introducing an English reflexive that is absent from the Syriac. This does not displace the embodied liturgical use of **“reverence”** for `nesġōḏ` in Ferial Slotha d'Ramsha Line 3, where **“We reverence Your Lordship”** also preserves the direct-object construction.
- **Teshbhotha l'Alaha Line 1 — decided 2026-08-24:** `tešḃōḥtā` is rendered **“Glorification”** in **“Glorification to God in the heights.”** The rendering makes the active praise/glorification force especially visible in the angelic acclamation. The same form remains **“glory”** in Our Father Line 14; the Glossary therefore records the contextual distinction rather than forcing one English gloss across both occurrences.
- **Psalm 11 Line 10 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `zadīqā` is rendered **“the Righteous One”** as a referent to God. This was settled after contextual and comparative study of the Psalm and is not to be reopened merely because the adjective can denote an ordinary righteous person elsewhere.
- **Psalm 11 Line 14 and Psalm 12 Lines 6, 8, and 10 — decided 2026-08-24:** the predicative active participles `bāḥar` and `mmallīn` are rendered with the English progressive — **“is examining”** and **“are speaking”** — to keep their ongoing participial force visible. This is not a mechanical rule for every Syriac participle: Psalm 11 Line 19 retains **“He loves righteousness”** because English normally treats “love” as stative rather than progressive.
- **Psalm 12 Line 1 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `ṭāḇā` is rendered **“good”** in “for good has come to an end.” The substantival adjective is understood here as the good rather than as a particular “good man,” which better fits the project’s reading of the clause.
- **Psalm 12 Line 7 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** `bsep̈wāṯā mp̈alḡāṯā` remains **“in divided lips.”** The wording deliberately preserves the Syriac `b-` image: a man speaks “in divided lips.”
- **Psalm 12 Lines 9, 15, and 19 — affirmed 2026-08-24:** the imperfect forms are rendered as ongoing present divine actions — **“destroys,” “bring about,” “keep”** — rather than automatically as future declarations. The project interprets their sense in this Psalm as present and continuing.
- **Psalm 12 Lines 20–22 — decided 2026-08-24:** the final unit is rendered **“Rescue me and deliver me for Eternity from this generation, / Wherein the wicked circle about and walk / As [at] the contemptible high place of the sons of Edom.”** The initial `d-` is taken as relative to “this generation”; `ʾaḏōm` is the canonical Syriac reading “Edom”; and the physical “high place” with circumambulation is supported by the Syriac traditional exegesis examined during translation.
- **Psalm 13 Line 3 — decided 2026-08-26:** `ʾeṯraʿā lī` is rendered **“Be reconciled to me.”** The rendering preserves the relational/reconciliatory force securely attested for the Ethpaal rather than flattening the clause to “consider me” or “be pleased with me.”
- **Psalm 13 Line 11 — decided 2026-08-26:** `zkēy^tēh` is rendered **“I have conquered him.”** The victory force of `z-k-ʾ` is kept visible and preserves resonance with the recurring same-root language of conquering in Revelation rather than defaulting to the broader English “overcome.”
